    <h1>About TrEnCh</h1>
    <p class="lead">The TrEnCh project is primarily supported by a National Science Foundation Rules of Life Grant to investigate the physiological and genetic mechanisms underlying grasshopper responses to climate change.</p>

<section class="section">
  <div class="container">
    <div style="display:grid;grid-template-columns:1.3fr 1fr;gap:4rem;align-items:start;">
      <div>
        <h2>Our approach</h2>
        <p>Many efforts to forecast ecological responses to climate change are based on air temperatures at coarse spatial (degrees) and temporal (monthly) resolutions. But animals experience their environment at much finer resolution—responding to changes in environmental conditions at the scale of minutes and meters.</p>
        <p>We provide computational and visualization tools that translate air temperature into usable metrics, to improve ecological forecasting related to climate change.</p>
        <p>Our model framework takes an integrative, additive approach that encompasses biochemical processes including enzyme kinetics and aerobic capacity, biophysical mechanisms including energy and water budgets and heat balance, fitness metrics such as growth, activity, and reproduction, population-level mechanisms including density dependence and dispersal, and evolutionary mechanisms including quantitative genetics and natural selection.</p>
        <div class="callout" style="margin-top:2rem;">
          <p>"One important step is linking physiology to organism fitness in an integrated, additive model. We will use this framework to inform the relative importance of various physiological and life history systems..."</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div>
        <div style="background:var(--color-surface-offset);border-radius:var(--radius-xl);padding:var(--space-8);">
          <h3 style="font-size:var(--text-lg);margin-bottom:var(--space-4);">NSF Rules of Life Grant</h3>
          <p style="font-size:var(--text-base);color:var(--color-text-muted);">An exciting component is examining the relative importance of physiological changes to genetics with climate change. The goal is to integrate physiology into an evolutionary modeling approach that can bridge ecology and evolution to inform climate change adaptation.</p>
          <a href="http://faculty.washington.edu/lbuckley" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="btn-trench" style="margin-top:1rem;">Visit the Buckley Lab</a>
        </div>
      </div>
